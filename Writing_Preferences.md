# Writing Preferences

## Voice
- No em dashes
- Terse, grounded, no filler
- Should sound like me talking, not like polished marketing copy
- Short sentences over compound ones when possible

## Structure
- One theme per piece, stated explicitly as a character statement
- Anchor around one specific scene or moment, not general accomplishments
- Lead with stakes/context, then the hard moment, then what I did, then result
- End with the principle I carry forward, then a crisp result line

## Content
- Concrete and specific over impressive-sounding and vague
- Real details (commit traces, SDK versions, actual decisions) over abstraction
- Do not invent or inflate — if there is not enough to write about, say so
- I am more than a programmer: emphasize leadership, judgment, compassion, hard work alongside technical ability

## What to Avoid
- Stopgap framing (do not frame workarounds as clever)
- Listing accomplishments without a through-line
- Generic "challenge/growth" arc with no specific moment
- AI-sounding prose, hedging language, or filler transitions

## Topic Preferences
- Prefer college experiences over high school for current applications (rising sophomore, Jul 2026)
- CyLab: theme is natural workflows over stopgaps, conviction under researcher pressure, root-cause thinking
- ScottyLabs: theme is humanitarian utility to developers over technical elegance
- Robotics (FTC, Captain, world-record team, first state appearance): strong story but feels dated now — use only if college experiences are insufficient
