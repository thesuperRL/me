### Common App Personal Essay
*Prompt: Some students have a background, identity, interest, or talent that is so meaningful they believe their application would be incomplete without it. If this sounds like you, please share your story.*

It's another morning in the office building.

Four people sit around a table, returning from a good night's rest. Discussions quiet down as the blinds rise and lights fall on the inhabitants. It's 8 am. The meeting in my mind is ready to begin, as is my day.

These four characters are sides of my personality: my sensitive nature, analytic skill, competitive desire, and collaborative spirit. Every moment, they debate my course of action.

I grew up with my sensitive nature, who revealed the world's influences on me. I was fixated on my feelings and often overthought, but self-reflection showed me I needed to change. My analytical skill emerged to defend me from these thoughts, dissecting the context and motivations behind actions to discern intentions. However, analysis began taking over my thinking, and I neglected others' feelings in games. Nobody had fun, as I'd always stick coldly to rules.

When I discovered my loss of friends, I reflected on my behavior. I began applying my sensitive nature to others, placing myself in their shoes to help me empathize. I made better-informed decisions using situational and emotional awareness, thus regaining my friendships.

Meanwhile, I began with my competitive desire, who strived to do my best in everything. I excelled in various subjects and would answer every question, receiving the praise of teachers. However, I began boasting about my accomplishments, bothering my classmates. Self-reflection showed me I needed to change. My collaborative spirit emerged, encouraging me to control my urge to show myself while giving others opportunities. Yet, as I refrained from contributing, our team projects decreased in quality.

When I realized that projects did poorly, I reflected on my teamwork. I began applying my competitive desire to others by aligning with their interests, empathizing with their struggles, and teaching with an awareness of their feelings. I strive to assign fitting roles based on interest, letting others contribute in enjoyable ways. Though I slow myself down by taking additional responsibilities, we gain more team synergy. We accelerated to end projects with better results and more enjoyment.

Last summer, these four sides came together as I led others in a large team. Wanting to broaden my scope from teaching EPS students to teaching beyond EPS, I founded a free online tutoring camp with nine tutors, teaching four subjects over three weeks. My collaborative spirit helped me scout volunteers among the most knowledgeable students at EPS. It arranged opportunities equally and designated a "course lead" for each course to organize the creation of each syllabus. My competitive desire taught every lesson other tutors couldn't. Its high standards pushed me to schedule daily calls with others to confirm progress. My analytic skill digested websites I collected to form PowerPoints and CAD demos. It examined sessions to give feedback to both tutors and courses. My sensitive nature helped me soften the words of a pragmatic, blunt team lead while strengthening the requests of one who was too easygoing. With the combined power of these four sides, the program was a fulfilling success, delivering informative lessons and receiving positive feedback.

When I reviewed the birth and evolution of my sides, I found a pattern in those two pairs. I started from a talent, was challenged, reflected, and gained new contrasting abilities. Reflections further showed me the benefits of my nature, which I applied to others using empathy.

Since then, I've added a "debrief" to each day's meeting. When brushing my teeth, my mind flies with reflections of the day's actions. I distribute feedback to each of my sides so that they avoid making similar mistakes.

Many new contrasting pairs have entered this ever-expanding conference room. Whether it is my talkative nature and active listening or my impulsive nature and patient demeanor, I continue to grow more inclusive and diverse. My doors will always be open to more sides, and I look forward to having them.
