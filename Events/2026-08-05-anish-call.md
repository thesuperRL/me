# The Anish Call
**Date:** 2026-08-05, 1:00-1:52 PM Pacific
**Context:** ScottyLabs DevOps and Internal Tooling Tech Lead

---

## What Happened

- Ryan returned from 15213 finals and a 2-day break to find Anish had already staged infrastructure changes (forgejo migration, port changes) that were Ryan's work to lead
- In the group chat, Ryan said "wait anish can you let me do this" and it was absorbed and ignored
- Described feeling "squeezed out of the org"
- Prepared a list of asks and brought them to a scheduled video call with Anish
- Went in dehydrated from the entire morning, physically lightheaded, and felt a tension he had not experienced before with Anish in prior conversations
- Call ran 1:00-1:52 PM Pacific

---

## What Was Prepared

Ryan's list going in:
1. He leads and coordinates the team
2. He owns the organizational changes he wants to implement
3. New infrastructure decisions need a real justification process before they start
4. Migrations should happen less frequently
5. Changes should be raised for discussion before being staged
6. Prioritizing unblocking others above making new changes

---

## The Hard Moment

- Mid-call, lost ability to form coherent thoughts
- Two simultaneous causes: not feeling in a position to make demands from Anish, and thoughts being half-formed going in
- First time he had felt this kind of tension with Anish specifically
- Usually stronger in real-time conversation than his conversational partner; this was an exception
- Physical state (dehydration, lightheadedness) compounded it

---

## What Was Agreed

- Ryan leads and coordinates the team: agreed
- New infrastructure decisions need a real justification process before starting: kinda agreed
- Friday meeting to go through all issues on the task board: agreed

---

## What Was Pushed Back On and Why Ryan Found It Convincing

- **Fewer migrations:** A lack of migrations causes necessary changes to stack up and eventually compound into a full rewrite being required. Ryan found this correct.
- **Raise changes for discussion first:** If implementation is delayed it could block others. Ryan found this correct.
- **Prioritize unblocking above own projects:** Anish does not want devops to become IT. Devops should have its own projects it can prioritize. Issues raised by other teams are typically problems with each project's own code that they assume is a devops problem. Ryan found this correct.

---

## Result

- Two verbal agreements
- Friday task board meeting
- No structural change yet
- Ryan disappointed at himself for how he performed, not at the outcome itself

---

## What Friday Needs to Produce

Ryan's own words on what needs to happen before Friday for this to have been worth it:
- Fully understand the codebase
- Have a list of potentially unnecessary things to bring up with Anish
- A much more concrete idea of the future direction

---

## Lesson Formalized

- Physical state is real context: dehydrated, lightheaded, and in a new kind of tension is not the time for a high-stakes conversation. If possible, reschedule. If not, name it briefly at the start.
- Two verbal agreements and a Friday meeting is not nothing. The outcome and how the conversation felt are separate things.
- When counterarguments are actually correct, updating your position is the right call, not a failure. Ryan did this three times in one call.
- The gap between preparation and execution under compromised conditions: the preparation was solid, the execution broke down. The lesson is not to prepare differently, it is to manage the conditions better.

---

## Core Theme: Showing Up Imperfectly and Still Getting Something

- Ryan's standard for himself is high enough that a messy but functional conversation feels like failure
- The two agreements are real. The Friday meeting is real. The updated positions on the three tactical points are real.
- The disappointment is at himself, which is the same pattern as always: hardest on himself about things he sees most clearly

---

## Potential Writing Angles

- The gap between how a conversation feels and what it accomplishes
- Performing under compromised conditions: what you can still get done when you are not at your best
- Updating your position when the counterarguments are correct: intellectual honesty as a form of strength, not concession
- The "squeezed out" feeling and what it takes to name it and act on it anyway
