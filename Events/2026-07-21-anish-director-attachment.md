# Anish's Attachment to the Stack: A Director Who Won't Let Go
**Date:** 2026-07-21 (ongoing; logs from this date)
**Context:** ScottyLabs DevOps and Internal Tooling Tech Lead

---

## What Happened

- Anish built the ScottyLabs infrastructure from scratch and is now Director of Tech
- As director, he is still the primary developer of the stack: making frequent unilateral changes (secretspec, moving devenv into kennel) without coordinating downstream impact
- Each change breaks things for other teams: devenv breaks, CI skips silently, tech leads can't get their local environments working
- Ryan absorbs the fallout each time
- Tech leads are venting frustration directly in the devops channel; Anish sees the messages and does not change course
- Anish's response to a complaint from the SLAI tech lead: "in the future projects will have to take care of their own infra issues lol"
- Ryan's read: "highly losing the 初心"

---

## The Hard Moment

- Not a single crux: a sustained pattern of friction across multiple incidents
- Ryan draws a clear line: no public callouts, DMs for things that actively counter their ideals or undermine his role
- Most direct action: 7/13/26 message to Anish demanding that team organization and task assignment flow through Ryan, not Anish
  - Ryan's exact words: "can you defer the organization of the devops team to me... this is my job and i want to do it"
  - Also: "if you are doing all the work in devops and organizing the 2-3 people in it while not really coordinating tech-level activity or ensuring succession in devops theres less evidence to vote you for director of slabs"
- Anish's response: acknowledged the structure but explained he couldn't find a clean handoff point while the infrastructure is still evolving
- What actually worked: building the project board made the structure visible enough that Anish stopped routing around Ryan on task assignment
- What didn't change: Anish's development pace and unilateral changes to the stack

---

## Reasoning at the Crux

- Ryan won't call Anish out publicly because it would hurt his reputation
- The threshold for a DM is when something actively counters their shared ideals or undermines Ryan's role directly
- Structural fixes (project board, ticket board) are preferred over repeated confrontation because they create durable accountability without requiring a fight each time
- Industry standard is a useful frame: "projects will take care of their own infra issues" is not inherently wrong; the timing and delivery were off given John was actively blocked
- Ryan's diagnosis of the root problem: "He has an attachment to this system since he first made it which is fair, but he has an issue with letting development flow independently from his direct control"
- The problem is not competence. It is that Anish built something he loves and the director role requires letting go of it.

---

## Result

- Task assignment issue resolved by the project board
- Broader pattern unchanged: frequent relearning of the stack, confusion across teams, tech leads hitting walls in their dev environments
- Ryan does not know what it would take for Anish to actually let go
- Watching it play out, not forcing it

---

## Lesson Formalized

- Structural fixes outlast confrontations: the project board worked where direct conversations did not
- There is a meaningful difference between the principle being right and the execution being right; "projects own their own infra" is sound, but not when said to someone who is actively blocked
- Diagnosing the root of a person's behavior (attachment, not incompetence) changes how you approach the problem
- Some things resolve themselves if you wait; some don't. The work is knowing which is which.

---

## Core Theme: Diagnosis Over Judgment

- Ryan's approach is to understand why Anish behaves this way, not just that he does
- That understanding (he loves what he built and can't let go) shapes the response: structural fixes that reduce his need to be involved, rather than direct pressure to change
- The 初心 principle applies to both of them: Anish has drifted from the purpose of the role; Ryan is trying not to

---

## Potential Writing Angles

- The person who built the system is often the last person who can let go of it: what that costs a team and how to manage around it
- Structural fixes as leadership: when you can't change a person, change the system they operate in
- The distinction between confrontation and accountability: Ryan is not avoiding conflict, he is choosing which conflicts to have and how
- Watching someone drift from their own 初心, and what you do when you can't force them back
